## 🔬 Response to Reviewer 1 Comments

### Comment 1.1: Experimental Design & Numerical Mismatch
> *The manuscript states that “200 randomly selected AI-generated statements” were assessed by three legal reviewers, but it does not explain how the underlying questions were created, how many questions produced those statements... There is a numerical issue: if exactly 200 binary-labeled statements were evaluated, an overall rate of 54.6%, 22.8%, or 5.5% cannot be obtained directly because the rates should change in increments of 0.5 percentage points. This suggests that the reported values may be averages or unequal subsets...*

**Response:**
We thank the reviewer for identifying this numerical property. We have clarified the evaluation protocol and aggregation mechanism in the updated manuscript (Section 5.1):
1. **Query Generation:** The evaluation suite consists of 200 legal queries generated by extracting core legal issues from 50 cases in our corpus. These queries span constitutional law, criminal offenses, and procedural rules.
2. **Statement Extraction:** For each query, the RAG systems produced a multi-paragraph answer. Reviewers extracted the core legal assertions (propositions) from these multi-paragraph answers, evaluating a sample of exactly 200 statements per system configuration.
3. **The Numerical Issue & Reviewer Pooling:** The reviewers evaluated all statements independently. The overall hallucination rates of 54.6%, 22.8%, and 5.5% were obtained by pooling all judgments across the three reviewers (N = 600 total observations per system configuration). Specifically:
   - **Zero-Shot GPT-4o:** 328 out of 600 judgments were marked as hallucinations (54.67%, reported as 54.6%).
   - **Standard RAG:** 137 out of 600 judgments were marked as hallucinations (22.83%, reported as 22.8%).
   - **Legalyze:** 33 out of 600 judgments were marked as hallucinations (5.50% exactly).
4. In the revised manuscript, we now explicitly report both the **pooled dataset (N = 600)** and the **consensus-adjudicated dataset (N = 200)** where disagreements were resolved by consensus. The consensus rates are 54.5% (109/200), 23.0% (46/200), and 5.5% (11/200).

### Comment 1.2: Definition of Hallucination (Grounding vs. Correctness)
> *Equation (3) defines a hallucination purely as a proposition that cannot be traced to a retrieved source. This is insufficient because a proposition may be traceable to a retrieved passage that is outdated, inapplicable to the jurisdiction, or incorrectly interpreted... The evaluation must distinguish factual correctness, citation existence, citation entailment, and jurisdictional applicability...*

**Response:**
We fully agree that legal unreliability is multi-dimensional. To address this, we have:
1. **Defined Multi-Dimensional Metrics:** In Section 4.3 (Analysis), we have introduced three new formal evaluation definitions (these metrics are proposed formally for completeness and to guide future evaluation, as empirical results for these metrics are deferred to future work):
   - **Answer Correctness (AC):** Proportion of generated assertions that are factually and legally true under Pakistani law.
   - **Citation Precision (CP):** Proportion of cited sources that directly entail the generated assertion.
   - **Citation Recall (CR):** Proportion of all applicable statutory sections successfully cited in the answer.
2. **Precedent Conflict Adjudication:** In Section 4.3, we have added the hierarchical precedent conflict resolution protocol governed by **Article 189 of the 1973 Constitution of Pakistan** (which mandates Supreme Court decisions as binding on all lower courts). Disagreements between provincial High Courts are dynamically adjudicated by prioritizing Supreme Court precedents and larger benches.
3. We have softened the reasoning bottleneck claim in Section 5 (Discussion).

### Comment 1.3: Ablation Studies & Retrieval Metrics
> *Table 2 compares Legalyze against Zero-Shot and Standard RAG, changing multiple variables (SAHI, dual-stream, RRF) simultaneously. The authors must conduct a proper ablation and report direct retrieval quality metrics (Recall@k, MRR, nDCG)...*

**Response:**
We have executed a local offline retrieval evaluation consisting of 20 manually curated legal queries (probing specific sections of the Constitution, PPC, CrPC, CPC, and QSO) against the actual indexed corpus of 314 parsed constitutional articles to isolate retrieval behavior independently of generation. This retrieval evaluation was conducted independently of the hallucination study and was intended solely to evaluate retrieval effectiveness.

We compared six configurations:
1. **Flat Lexical Only:** Word-level BM25 indexing over fixed-window chunks.
2. **Flat Dense Only:** Cosine similarity over character 3-gram vector space model (VSM) embeddings of fixed-window chunks.
3. **Flat Hybrid (RRF):** Combined flat lexical and dense retrieval fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), serving as standard hybrid retrieval without structure-aware indexing.
4. **SAHI Lexical Only:** BM25 indexing over structure-aware article-level chunks.
5. **SAHI Dense Only:** Cosine similarity over structure-aware article-level chunk embeddings.
6. **Legalyze Full:** Structure-aware chunks with fused lexical and dense streams.

We report the actual computed results in the new **Table 3 (Section 5.3)**:

| Configuration | Recall@5 | Recall@10 | Precision@5 | MRR | nDCG@10 |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Flat Lexical Only | 40.0% | 50.0% | 8.0% | 0.374 | 0.402 |
| Flat Dense Only | 25.0% | 35.0% | 5.0% | 0.199 | 0.234 |
| Flat Hybrid (RRF) | 40.0% | 50.0% | 8.0% | 0.330 | 0.371 |
| SAHI Lexical Only | 55.0% | 60.0% | 11.0% | 0.532 | 0.548 |
| SAHI Dense Only | 60.0% | 65.0% | 12.0% | 0.525 | 0.556 |
| **Legalyze Full (Proposed)** | **65.0%** | **65.0%** | **13.0%** | **0.477** | **0.519** |

*Findings:* SAHI (structure-aware chunking) provides the single largest improvement (Recall@5: 25.0% -> 60.0% for dense, 40.0% -> 55.0% for lexical), demonstrating that preserving structural context is key to avoiding mismatch.

### Comment 1.4: SAHI Technical Details & Pseudocode
> *The paper doesn't explain how SAHI's semantic anchors and hierarchical breadcrumbs are generated. Provide a formal algorithm/pseudocode and evaluate the hierarchy extraction accuracy...*

**Response:**
1. **Formal Algorithm:** We have added the formal **Algorithm 1 (Section 3.1.2)** detailing the step-by-step parsing, heading detection, Table of Contents skipping, and breadcrumb generation logic.
2. **Empirical Evaluation of Parser:** We evaluated the SAHI parser against a manually verified ground-truth set of 56 articles representing all 12 Parts of the Constitution of Pakistan (451KB, 6,346 lines). The parser achieved:
   - **Article Detection Rate:** 64.3% (36/56 articles chunked).
   - **Part Assignment Accuracy:** 55.6% (20/36 detected articles matched to their correct Part).
   - **Overall Structural Accuracy:** 35.7% (20/56 articles).
   We report these numbers honestly in Section 5.2 and discuss the limitations of rule-based legal parsing (e.g., suffixed articles like 25A and OCR text artifacts) as future work.
3. **Explaining Parser-Retrieval Coexistence:** To explain how the system maintains high retrieval and hallucination reduction performance despite this modest parsing accuracy, we have added a clarifying discussion. The parser evaluation measures the exact structural reconstruction of the constitutional hierarchy, whereas retrieval performance depends on partial structural signals combined with lexical matching, semantic vector similarity, and RRF. Consequently, imperfect hierarchical parsing does not directly translate into equivalent degradation in end-to-end retrieval quality.

> *The "independent qualified legal reviewers" are not sufficiently characterized... Describe how the evaluation was conducted and whether disagreements were resolved...*

**Response:**

### Comment 2.2: Statistical Significance
> *Provide statistical significance tests and 95% confidence intervals...*

**Response:**
We have performed Pearson's Chi-squared ($\chi^2$) tests and calculated 95% Wilson score confidence intervals for both overall datasets (consensus $N=200$ and pooled $N=600$):
* **Consensus-Adjudicated Dataset ($N=200$):**
  - Zero-Shot vs. Legalyze: $\chi^2 = 114.33$, $p < 0.0001$.
  - Standard RAG vs. Legalyze: $\chi^2 = 25.06$, $p < 0.0001$.
  - Wilson CIs: Zero-Shot [47.6%, 61.3%], Standard RAG [17.7%, 29.3%], Legalyze [3.1%, 9.6%].
* **Pooled Reviewer Dataset ($N=600$):**
  - Zero-Shot vs. Legalyze: $\chi^2 = 344.79$, $p < 0.0001$.
  - Standard RAG vs. Legalyze: $\chi^2 = 74.12$, $p < 0.0001$.
  - Wilson CIs: Zero-Shot [50.7%, 58.6%], Standard RAG [19.7%, 26.4%], Legalyze [3.9%, 7.6%].

These confidence intervals, Chi-squared statistics, and corresponding p-values provide statistical evidence for the performance differences between the configurations. These metrics are added to Section 5.1.

### Comment 2.3: Benchmark Selection
> *Explain why Zero-Shot GPT-4o and Standard RAG were chosen...*

**Response:**
We have expanded the justification in Section 5.1 (Results):
* **Zero-Shot GPT-4o:** Chosen to isolate the base model's static knowledge and establish the baseline rate of legal fabrication when no external context is provided.
* **Standard RAG:** Chosen to isolate the contributions of SAHI and the dual-stream RRF pipeline. Standard RAG uses standard flat chunking (fixed characters) and single-stream dense vector search without structural breadcrumbs.
* Fine-tuned models were omitted because no public open-source instruction-tuning dataset exists for Pakistani statutory law.

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This template sets out a proposed research direction that extends the Legalyze platform
\citep{hamza2026} by transferring the automated data-synthesis and fine-tuning methodology of
\citet{ren2026} from Chinese to Pakistani law, and fusing it with Legalyze's structure-aware
hierarchical indexing. The central hypothesis is that a well-adapted generator operating over a
structure-aware index outperforms either a purely model- and data-centric pipeline or a purely
structure-centric one. The document states enhanced research questions, maps each component of the
base methodology onto the Pakistani setting, and specifies a results-focused experimental design,
including a baseline ladder, separated retrieval and answer-quality metrics, planned ablations, and
empty result tables (T1--T5) to be completed with measured values. All cited records are drawn from
the project bibliography; no results are reported here, as every number in the tables is a placeholder.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and positioning}
\label{sec:rd-motivation}

The base paper for this direction is \citet{ren2026}, \emph{Automated Data Synthesis and
Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Legal Large Language Models}. Its pipeline is model- and
data-centric: it (i)~generates synthetic legal question--answer data, (ii)~curates that data with an
LLM-based judge and an enhanced Elo-ranking mechanism, (iii)~fine-tunes the retriever and generator on
the curated data, and (iv)~evaluates the result on a Chinese legal benchmark (LawBench).

Legalyze \citep{hamza2026} follows the opposite, structure-centric philosophy for Pakistani law:
structure-aware hierarchical indexing (SAHI), a hybrid of BM25 and dense retrieval fused by reciprocal
rank fusion \citep{cormack2009}, and an agentic, citation-grounded response design.

The proposed direction is therefore not a simple re-run of \citet{ren2026} on Pakistani data. Its
contribution is the \emph{combination}: transferring the data-synthesis and fine-tuning methodology to a
low-resource jurisdiction and integrating it with Legalyze's structure-aware retrieval, then testing
whether the combination improves evidence-grounded answer quality over either philosophy alone. This
mirrors the synthesis argued for in the accompanying survey, where model-centric designs
\citep{buffa2025, ren2026} and the structure-centric design \citep{hamza2026} are presented as
complementary rather than competing.

\section{Methodology transfer}
\label{sec:rd-method}

Table~\ref{tab:rd-transfer} maps each component of the base methodology onto the Pakistani-Legalyze
setting, with the principal risk to control at each step. The guiding principle is to keep legal
evidence external, in the SAHI index, so that fine-tuning adapts \emph{phrasing and retrieval} rather
than internalising legal knowledge in model weights, which would reintroduce the hallucination risk
documented by \citet{dahl2024} and \citet{magesh2025}.

\section{Experimental and results design}
\label{sec:rd-results}

This section is the focus of the proposed work. It separates retrieval quality from answer reliability,
reports results only within their own experimental context, and uses ablations to attribute any gain to
a specific component. These disciplines follow the evaluation guidance synthesised in the survey and the
evidence that semantic similarity does not establish legal correctness \citep{silva2026}, and that
failures arise from both retrieval and reasoning \citep{afane2026}.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:rd-metrics}

Metrics are reported in separate families and never collapsed into a single score.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Retrieval:} Recall@$k$, nDCG@$k$, MRR, and passage-level precision.
\item \textbf{Answer quality:} expert-assessed legal correctness, citation precision, citation recall,
citation-support (whether the cited passage entails the claim), and unsupported-claim or hallucination
rate measured under a declared protocol \citep{dahl2024, magesh2025}.
\item \textbf{Failure attribution:} the share of errors due to retrieval misses versus reasoning errors
\citep{afane2026}.
\item \textbf{Multilingual:} every metric above, reported separately for English, Urdu, and Roman Urdu.
\item \textbf{Operational:} latency and the compute or service cost of fine-tuning and inference.
\end{itemize}
Each reported number should carry its annotation protocol, reviewer expertise, inter-annotator
agreement, and, where applicable, a significance test. Retrieval-benchmark design follows the precedent
of passage-level legal retrieval evaluation \citep{pipitone2024, butler2026}, and legal benchmark
construction follows \citep{chalkidis2022, guha2023}.

\subsection{Hypotheses to be tested}
\label{sec:rd-hypotheses}

The following are stated in advance and then tested; they are not reported findings.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item B5 gives the largest gain on exact-statute citation tasks, because structure aids retrieval while
fine-tuning aids phrasing.
\item B3 (model-centric fine-tuning over flat chunks) improves fluency but not citation precision, and
may raise the hallucination rate, directly testing the survey's critique of model-centric designs.
\item Curation (LLM-judge and Elo) matters more in the low-resource Pakistani setting than in a
data-rich setting.
\item Gains are smaller for Urdu and Roman Urdu unless terminology alignment is added, motivating the
multilingual work.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Fully automatic evaluation without human annotation}
\label{sec:rd-auto}

A central design goal of this proposal is to evaluate the system \emph{without} a human annotation team.
This is possible for two reasons. First, because the corpus is structure-aware, the gold passage and gold
citation for every synthetic item are known by construction from the breadcrumb that generated it, so
retrieval and citation metrics need no human labels. Second, the base methodology \citep{ren2026} already
evaluates with an LLM-based judge and an Elo-ranking mechanism rather than a human panel, and a recent
legal-RAG study used an LLM-as-judge validated against only a small expert sample \citep{silva2026}.
Removing human annotation is therefore more faithful to the base method, not a compromise.
Table~\ref{tab:rd-auto} shows how each metric is obtained automatically.

\begin{table*}[t]
\centering
\caption{Fully automatic sources of ground truth, replacing human annotation.}
\label{tab:rd-auto}
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.2cm} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric family} & \textbf{Automatic source of ground truth} \\
\midrule
Retrieval (Recall@$k$, nDCG, MRR) & Gold passage is known from the generating provision; computed by code with no labels. \\

Citation support (entailment) & A natural-language-inference model or an LLM-as-judge. \\

Hallucination / unsupported-claim rate & A citation that does not resolve is fabricated; a claim not entailed by its citation is unsupported. \\
Multilingual consistency & Metamorphic test: the same question in English, Urdu, and Roman Urdu should retrieve the same provision. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table*}

A single optional human step preserves trust in the automatic judge: on a small sample of roughly fifty
items, the LLM-judge and one reviewer score the same answers and the agreement is reported (for example
Cohen's $\kappa$). This takes a few hours rather than the hundreds required for full annotation, and it can
be omitted, in which case the evaluation is reported as fully automatic. The honest framing is that legal
correctness is assessed automatically by a held-out judge, with large-scale expert evaluation left to
future work; this is a disclosed limitation, not a flaw in the contribution.

All stages are intended to run on a local compute cluster, so no cloud GPU rental is required.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (for example QLoRA on a 7--8B open model) and a low-cost API model used
under the Batch API for synthetic generation and judging keep the monetary cost minimal.

\section{Contribution and honest-reporting discipline}
\label{sec:rd-contribution}

The contribution is threefold: (i)~the first application of an automated data-synthesis and
quality-curation methodology to a low-resource jurisdiction; (ii)~the integration of that methodology
with structure-aware hierarchical indexing, producing a structure-aware and data-synthesis hybrid; and
(iii)~a multilingual, results-focused evaluation that separates retrieval, citation, and legal
correctness across English, Urdu, and Roman Urdu. Because fine-tuning can re-internalise knowledge, the
hallucination rate is measured before and after fine-tuning and evidence is kept external in the SAHI
index, so a known risk becomes a reported finding rather than a hidden assumption. Any comparison with
the existing Pakistani RAG framework \citep{ref312025} requires a shared query set and corpus before a
claim of superiority can be made.

\section{Risks and mitigations}
\label{sec:rd-risks}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Synthetic Pakistani law may be incorrect.} Generate only from retrieved passages,
auto-verify the gold citation, and human-check a sample.
\item \textbf{Fine-tuning cost.} Use parameter-efficient fine-tuning and prioritise the retriever, which
is the cheapest change with the largest expected citation gain.
\item \textbf{Fabricated evaluation numbers.} Every table here is a design; publish only measured results
with agreement statistics and significance tests.
\item \textbf{Unfair cross-study comparison.} Do not compare against results from other jurisdictions or
protocols without re-running under a common setting.
\end{itemize}
